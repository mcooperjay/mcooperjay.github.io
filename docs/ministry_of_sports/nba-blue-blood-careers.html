Does College Pedigree Predict NBA Career Length?

A Bayesian comparison of ‘blue blood’ and non-blue-blood alumni

Author

Cooper Maughan (with Jonas Schiel)

Published

December 1, 2025

Overview

“Blue blood” college basketball programs — Duke, North Carolina, UCLA, Indiana, Kentucky, and Kansas — are historically dominant and heavily recruited from. The question: does attending one of these programs actually predict a longer NBA career, or is that reputation not backed up by the data? This matters to a recruit choosing a school and to a front office comparing two draft prospects.

Data & Method

Using publicly available NBA player records (Kaggle), we computed each player’s total years played and split the sample into blue-blood alumni and everyone else. Career length is a non-negative, discrete, right-skewed variable (most careers are short; a few are very long), which makes a Poisson-Gamma conjugate model a natural fit — the Poisson likelihood models the count of years played, and the Gamma prior (informed via data augmentation, with different shape parameters for each group reflecting our prior expectation of ~5 years for blue-blood alumni vs. ~3.5 for others) yields a closed-form posterior.

We were explicit about the model’s main limitation: years played within a single career aren’t truly independent (a player who’s already played 5 seasons is far more likely to play a 6th than a rookie is to play a 2nd), which the model can’t capture. We treated this as a known simplification rather than pretending the assumption held.

Results

  • 95% credible interval for mean career length: (5.09, 5.51) years for blue-blood alumni vs. (4.27, 4.40) years for everyone else
  • 95% credible interval for the difference in means: (0.74, 1.19) years — meaning there’s a 95% posterior probability the true gap falls in that range
  • Posterior predictive probability of a career longer than 5 years: 42.8% for blue-blood alumni vs. 27.1% for others
  • Posterior predictive probability of a career longer than 10 years: 1.8% vs. 0.76%

The data support a real gap: blue-blood alumni have measurably longer expected NBA careers.

Limitations

Career length is only one definition of “success” — it says nothing about points per game, awards, or team accomplishments. The “blue blood” label is also static; some programs on that list (UCLA, for instance) have struggled in recent years and arguably shouldn’t be grouped with currently-dominant programs. A follow-up analysis could control for draft position, era, and position, or use a more current definition of blue-blood status.

Skills demonstrated: Bayesian conjugate modeling (Poisson-Gamma), credible interval interpretation, posterior predictive probability, explicit reasoning about violated modeling assumptions.
